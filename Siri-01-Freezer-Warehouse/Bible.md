## 1. Main character (recurring across ALL series)

**Cal Mercer**, early 30s, temp-agency night-shift worker. Takes whatever overnight assignment the agency gives him. That's the channel concept: every series is a different shift that never ends.

- **Personality:** quiet, practical, counts everything (minutes, steps, cans). Dry humour under pressure. Not a fighter; he survives by noticing things.
- **Flaw:** avoids responsibility for other people. He always takes the job, never the lead. Each series forces him to lead anyway.
- **Signature items (always visible; matches the channel logo and banner):**
  1. **SIGNATURE ITEM (never change):** a battered black handheld tactical radio with a distinctive orange display, a long antenna and worn tape strips around its body. It's always clipped to his chest strap or held in his hand.
  2. A **red cloth armband tied around his LEFT upper arm**. It belonged to his late brother **Danny** (a runner who read survival books and wore it on his wrist), and he never takes it off. Danny's rules: *keep moving, stay dry, count everything.* The cheek scar is an old box-cutter cut from a cardboard factory job, not an infection scratch (established in Ep 2).
  3. A weathered brown survival jacket over a dark hooded layer, charcoal cargo pants, dark tactical boots, fingerless gloves, a rugged dark survival backpack
  4. A black digital wristwatch, a story prop he uses to count days and minutes
- **Expressions to use:** fear, exhaustion, suspicion, urgency, quiet determination. He's an ordinary man, not a superhero.

## 2. Location: Harlan Cold Logistics

A frozen-food distribution warehouse on an industrial road 9 km outside the city of **Ridgeport**, next to the highway.

| Zone | Temperature | What's there | Story use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loading dock** | Outside temp | 6 bay doors (Bay 4 is broken and won't fully close), one parked 18-wheeler | Main entry point for the infected |
| **Ambient warehouse** | Room temp | Racks of dry goods, forklift charging bay, pallet wrap, tools | Danger zone: warm, so the infected move freely |
| **Chiller room** | +2°C | Dairy, vegetables, sliding door | Middle ground: the infected are slowed but still dangerous |
| **Deep freeze** | −22°C | 4,000 pallets of frozen food, heavy insulated door with a small window, no lock on the inside | Safe from them, but it slowly kills you |
| **Plant room** | Warm | **Ammonia** refrigeration system | Ticking bomb: a leak is deadly and powers everything cold |
| **Mezzanine office** | Room temp | Overlooks the warehouse floor, desktop PC, landline, radio base station, vending machine | Command post, and Dee and Marco's hideout |
| **Locker room** | Room temp | **6 insulated freezer suits**, first aid kit | Key resource: lets them stay in the freezer for hours |
| **Generator shed** | Outside | Diesel backup generator, 3 days of fuel at full load | Countdown: when power dies, the freezer warms up |
| **Roof hatch** | Outside | Ladder from the mezzanine | View of the highway and city, possible escape route |

## 3. Rules of the threat ("the turned")

Never call them zombies on screen. Survivors call them **"the turned."** The word "zombie" goes only in titles and tags, for search.

1. **Cause:** a bite or a deep scratch. Victims die in **1–3 hours**, then rise **10–40 minutes** later.
2. **They hunt heat.** They follow body heat and warm air, then sound. They walk past cold objects and cold rooms.
3. **Cold slows them.** At +2°C they are sluggish. Below about −10°C they stiffen and stop within minutes. **They do not die in the cold, they pause.** Warm them up and they wake.
4. **Noise travels.** A reversing alarm or a generator can draw a crowd from the highway.
5. **They don't open doors**, but they push. Enough of them can force a sliding door.
6. **No gore on screen.** Violence is implied, through sound, shadows and aftermath. This keeps the channel advertiser-friendly.

**Core tension of the series:** the cold that keeps the turned away is the same cold that is slowly killing Cal.

## 4. Side characters

- **Dolores "Dee" Okafor**, 58, night supervisor. Twenty years at Harlan. By-the-book, calm, knows every inch of the building. **Secret:** she was scratched in Episode 1 and is hiding it.
- **Marco Reyes**, 19, forklift driver, first job. Brave but impulsive, talks too much on the radio. Becomes Cal's responsibility.
- **Ray Dunn**, 47, truck driver. Arrives bitten at the start of Episode 1. He is the first to turn, and his truck's reversing alarm draws the crowd.
- **Later: the voice on the radio.** A woman called **Juno**, broadcasting from somewhere in Ridgeport from Episode 5 onwards. Ally or bait? The answer comes in Episode 11.

## 7. Episode writing rules (for every episode)

- 1,400–1,700 words, first person, present tense
- First 15 seconds: drop straight into danger, then state the stakes. No intro.
- Recap the previous cliffhanger in no more than 2 sentences.
- A new problem, discovery or decision every 60–90 seconds
- Concrete numbers: temperature, minutes on Cal's watch, number of bottles, metres
- Mark scene breaks with `[SCENE: short visual description for image generation]`
- End every episode with a cliffhanger
- Never reuse an escape method or a scare that has already been used
