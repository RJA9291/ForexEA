# Episode 3: Day 3
**Series:** Zombie Outbreak: 30 Days Trapped in a Freezer Warehouse

> Nombor dan masa ditulis dalam perkataan supaya suara AI membacanya dengan betul.

---

[SCENE: Close-up of a gloved hand flat against a fogged locker room door window from the inside, a dark silhouette behind the glass, Cal standing frozen on the corridor side, orange radio glow on his chest]

Last time, I opened the code lock to the locker room and found a hand on the inside of the glass. The freezer suits are in there. So is whatever left that hand.

Two twenty A.M. Behind me, the corridor fills with slow, wet footsteps from the chiller. In front of me, the hand slides down the glass and drops out of sight.

[SCENE: Cal pressing the radio button with his thumb, crouched against the corridor wall beside the steel door, looking back down the dim corridor toward the chiller]

"Marco. What's in the locker room?"

"No camera in there. Sorry, man. Privacy rules."

Of course. The one room I need, and it's the one room nobody's allowed to watch.

"How many behind me?"

"Two in the corridor. The puffer jacket guy and a woman. Moving slow. You've got maybe a minute."

[SCENE: A dim concrete corridor seen from Cal's position, two turned strangers (a man in a red puffer jacket and a woman in a long coat, NOT Dee or Marco) shuffling slowly toward the camera under a flickering tube light]

A minute. I think about Danny. Keep moving. Count everything.

Here's what I count. Something warm in front of me. Two slow ones behind me. One open shutter into a freezer at minus twenty-two, eighty metres back the way I came.

The cold stopped Lena. The cold can stop anything.

I don't need to fight them. I need to bring them somewhere cold.

[SCENE: Cal yanking the locker room door open, a turned night security guard in a dark uniform lunging out of the darkness toward him, Cal already turning to run]

I pull the locker room door open, and I run before I see what's inside.

I see it anyway, over my shoulder. A security guard. Dark uniform, a lanyard, a flashlight still clipped to his belt. His name tag catches the light. Walt.

He comes out of that room fast. Room temperature fast.

[SCENE: Cal sprinting back down the narrow corridor toward the two slow turned strangers, ducking low between them as they reach for him, the security guard chasing behind]

I run straight at the two slow ones. It's the stupidest plan I've ever had.

They're slow from the chiller, still half frozen. I duck between them, low, and they grab at the air where I was a second ago.

Walt is not slow. Walt is right behind me.

[SCENE: Cal bursting through the side door back into the green-lit chiller, sliding on the wet milk-covered floor between dairy racks, the security guard crashing through the door behind him]

Back through the side door, into the chiller. My boots hit the spilled milk and I almost go down.

Plus two degrees. Behind me, Walt hits the cold air and stumbles. Not much. Just a little. Like a runner who hit a hill.

It's enough. I gain three metres.

[SCENE: Cal diving under the half-raised grey roller shutter back into the blue freezer, the security guard right behind him reaching under the shutter]

The shutter. Sixty centimetres. I dive under it on my stomach and slide across the ice into the freezer.

Minus twenty-two. The cold hits me like a door slammed in my face.

Walt comes under after me.

[SCENE: Inside the blue freezer, the security guard on his hands and knees halfway into the room, slowing, frost already forming on his uniform shoulders, Cal backing away along the floor]

He gets halfway. He's on his hands and knees, reaching.

Then he slows. Then he slows more. Frost spreads across his shoulders like someone is painting it on.

Ten seconds. Twenty. His fingers stop a metre from my boot.

I scramble back to the shutter crank, out of his reach, and I hold my breath. I don't give him anything warm.

[SCENE: Cal leaning on the crank handle beside the roller shutter, breathing heavily, the frozen security guard motionless on the freezer floor, LENA visible frozen mid-step far down the aisle behind]

Two frozen now. Walt on the floor. Lena down in aisle six, still with one foot in the air.

I've just built a jail. The only door is a sixty-centimetre gap and a hand crank.

I crawl back under into the chiller and wind the shutter down until it touches the floor. Every click, I watch for movement. None.

[SCENE: The locker room with its door wide open, a row of grey lockers, six thick insulated freezer suits hanging on hooks, a dropped flashlight on the floor casting a beam across the tiles]

The locker room is empty now. Six freezer suits on six hooks, just like Dee said. Thick, insulated, rated to minus forty.

I put one on over everything. It's like climbing into a sleeping bag. My hands stop shaking for the first time since midnight.

I take two more, for Dee and Marco. And Walt's flashlight, from the floor.

There's a photo taped inside Walt's locker. A man in the same uniform, younger, holding a fish on a lake. A kid beside him holding a bigger fish.

I don't know why I look at it for so long. I close the locker door gently, like he might mind.

[SCENE: Cal in a bulky dark insulated freezer suit, red armband tied over the outside of the suit sleeve, climbing a metal staircase to the mezzanine with two folded suits under his arm, flashlight in hand]

Two fifty A.M. The two slow ones are still wandering the chiller, lost. I go the long way, around the racks, up the back stairs to the mezzanine.

Before I go up, I move the red cloth from my jacket to the outside of the suit. Danny's not riding under three layers of padding.

The office door opens before I knock.

[SCENE: The mezzanine office doorway, MARCO pulling Cal inside by the suit collar, DEE sitting in an office chair behind him wrapped in a coat, looking pale, monitors glowing on the desk]

Marco pulls me in by the collar and locks the door behind me. He's shaking. He's grinning. He looks about fifteen.

"You trapped them. You trapped two of them in the freezer. That was insane, man."

Dee doesn't get up. She's in her chair, wrapped in her coat, sleeve pulled down to her knuckles.

"Good work, Cal," she says. Her voice sounds like she's been running.

I hand out the suits. Marco puts his on in about four seconds. Dee takes hers and lays it across her lap and doesn't put it on.

"Phones?" I ask.

"Dead network since midnight," Marco says. "The landline rings out. I've tried nine-one-one maybe fifty times. I tried my mom twice as many."

He says it fast, like if he says it fast enough it won't land. I don't ask where his mom lives. He doesn't offer.

[SCENE: A whiteboard on the office wall with three rules written in black marker and a rough floor plan of the warehouse sketched beside it, Cal pointing at it]

We do what Dee always does when something goes wrong on a shift. We write it on the whiteboard.

One. They hunt heat first.

Two. With no heat close enough, they follow sound.

Three. Cold slows them. Below minus ten, they stop. They don't die. They wait.

Marco adds a fourth one underneath, in red. Don't drop the milk.

[SCENE: The office window overlooking the dark warehouse floor, dozens of turned silhouettes standing motionless in the loading dock area below, faint orange light from the open bay door]

Below the office window, the warehouse floor is full of them.

Thirty, maybe forty. Most of them standing still in the dock, near the trucks, near the warm engines. Waiting for something warm to walk past.

Nobody's walking past. Nobody's leaving. Bay Four is still open and nobody is going down there to close it.

[SCENE: Close-up of DEE's forearm as her sleeve slips back, a thin dark scratch visible on her skin for a split second, her hand quickly pulling the sleeve down again]

Dee reaches for her water. Her sleeve slides back an inch.

There's a mark on her forearm. Thin. Dark. Exactly where Ray grabbed her on the stairs.

She pulls the sleeve back down before I can be sure. She doesn't look at me.

I don't say anything. Not yet. I count instead. Ray, bitten at Exit Nine, dead in under two hours. Dee was scratched at one oh five. It's three A.M.

[SCENE: DEE in her office chair, sweating but wrapped in a coat, eyes closed, MARCO asleep curled on the floor under the desk with a freezer suit as a blanket, Cal sitting by the window watching her]

She's still here. She's tired, she's pale, but she's still Dee.

Marco falls asleep on the floor under a freezer suit. Dee closes her eyes in her chair.

I stay awake. I watch the floor. I watch Dee breathe. If she stops, I need to know before Marco does.

Around four, she opens her eyes and catches me watching.

"You can ask," she says quietly.

"Are you okay?"

"No," she says. "But I'm not Ray either. Not yet. Let me be the supervisor a little longer, Cal."

She closes her eyes again. I keep counting her breaths. Fourteen a minute. Then twelve. Then fourteen again.

[SCENE: The view from the office window at dawn, grey light through the high warehouse skylights falling on the motionless crowd in the dock below]

She doesn't stop. The sky through the skylights turns grey, then white.

Day three. Six A.M. The turned are still standing in the dock, in the same places, like a crowd at a bus stop that's given up on the bus.

Then the lights flicker.

[SCENE: The warehouse ceiling lights flickering off, the office plunged into darkness, Cal standing up quickly, monitors going black, only the orange radio glow on his chest]

Every light in the building goes out at once. The monitors die. The hum of the freezer compressors, the sound I stopped hearing hours ago, stops.

Silence. Total silence.

And then, from outside the building, a cough. A mechanical cough. Then a roar.

[SCENE: Exterior of the warehouse at dawn, a large diesel backup generator in a fenced shed coughing black smoke as it starts, its vibration shaking rain off the fence]

The backup generator. It kicks on, and the lights come back, and the compressors start humming again.

The freezer stays cold. Walt and Lena stay frozen. For a second I'm grateful.

Then I hear what Marco hears, sitting up from the floor, eyes wide.

That generator is loud. Very loud. The loudest thing for kilometres.

Marco gets it before I do. "Oh no. Oh, no, no, no."

On a normal night, you'd never notice that sound over the highway. But there is no highway anymore. No trucks, no sirens, no planes. Just the city going quiet, and in the middle of all that quiet, one diesel engine, roaring like a dinner bell.

[SCENE: Below the office window, every turned figure in the loading dock slowly turning its head toward the generator shed sound, some starting to shuffle out through the open bay door]

Rule two. With no heat close enough, they follow sound.

Down in the dock, forty heads turn at the same time. Toward the generator. Toward the noise.

Some of them start walking out through Bay Four. Out there, on the highway, I can see more shapes. Turning. Coming.

[SCENE: Close-up of a fuel gauge on a wall panel in the office, the needle near the top, a handwritten label taped beside it reading GENERATOR DIESEL with a scribbled note]

Dee opens her eyes and points at a panel on the wall. A fuel gauge, with a handwritten label.

"That generator runs everything," she says. "The lights. The compressors. The cold."

"How long?"

"Full load? Three days."

Three days of cold. Three days of a dinner bell ringing for every one of them within a kilometre.

And when the diesel runs out, the freezer warms up, and everything we've frozen wakes up.

*End of Episode 3.*
